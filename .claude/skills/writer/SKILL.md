# Writer: texts people read

The reader is not financial and reads the text once, in passing. Every text must make sense on its own, at that spot, to someone who has never seen the feature.

## Before writing
1. Understand what the thing does, step by step, with a real example (made-up amounts).
2. Write down, in one plain sentence, what the person needs to know at this spot. Write that, nothing more.

## Rules
- **Say what happens, to what, and when.** Name the money, the jar, the day. "On the 1st, what you didn't spend last month goes into the jars." Not "the leftover is applied".
- **No sentence that only makes sense if you know how the code works.** Bad: "Money that comes in this month waits for next month" (money doesn't wait anywhere). Good: "Your salary this month is used for your jars on the 1st of next month, if you don't spend it."
- **Every choice explains both sides.** A switch or a list says what happens with each option, not only the one that is on.
- **Context first.** A label says what it is about ("Money that fills the jars"), then the options. A tooltip starts with its subject.
- **Plain words.** No jargon (leftover, allocate, reserve, buffer, cycle, balance) unless explained in the same text. No "we"/"our".
- **Name things exactly.** Use the names on the screen in bold. Never "the box below" or "this setting".
- **Short.** One idea per line. Tooltips: a title line, then 1–3 short lines or bullets.
- **Same meaning in both languages.** Write the Hebrew as Hebrew, not a word-for-word copy; plural "you" forms as the rest of the app; check it reads right to left.
- **Numbers in examples** are made up and add up.

## Check before saving
Read each text as a new user who sees only this screen:
- Do I know what will happen to my money, and when?
- Is there any word I would have to guess?
- Does each option say what it does?
If any answer is no, rewrite.
